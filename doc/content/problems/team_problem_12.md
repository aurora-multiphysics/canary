# TEAM Problem 12: The FELIX Cantilevered Beam

[TEAM](https://www.compumag.org/wp/team/) Problem 12 is a coupled electromagnetic and mechanical benchmark
from the COMPUMAG workshop series, based on experiments on the FELIX facility at Argonne National Laboratory.
An aluminium plate, clamped at one end, sits in two crossed magnetic fields: a steady field $B_x$ along its
length and a field $B_y$ normal to its broad face that decays exponentially. The decaying field induces eddy
currents circulating in the plate; where they cross the plate's width they also cross $B_x$, and the resulting
Lorentz force bends the beam.

What makes the problem a test of coupling rather than of either physics alone is that the beam's motion
through $B_x$ induces a motional EMF, $\vec{v} \times \vec{B}$, which drives currents opposing the motion. The
stronger the steady field, the more the beam is damped: at $B_x = 0.2$ T it rings at its natural frequency,
while at $B_x = 0.9$ T it makes one swing and settles. Ignoring the coupling would predict a deflection
proportional to $B_x$; the measured peak deflection barely changes between 0.5 and 0.9 T.

This example uses the MFEM backend of MOOSE, so it requires an application built with MFEM support. It is
skipped by `./run_tests` when that capability is absent. It also uses `MFEMTimeDerivativeVectorMassKernel`,
`MFEMVectorMassKernel` and `MFEMVectorFEDomainLFCurlKernel`, so the MOOSE build needs to include those.

## Problem Specification id=specification

The geometry is that of Fig. 15 of Turner et al. (1988) and the parameters are those of the LSTC test case
document for the problem (EM-VAL-3.1, 2012), which also reproduces the measurements.

| Quantity | Value |
| :- | :- |
| Beam | 487 mm ($x$) by 3.175 mm ($y$) by 100 mm ($z$) |
| Clamped length | 76 mm at the root, leaving 411 mm free |
| Material | aluminium: $\sigma = 25.32 \times 10^{6}$ S/m, $\rho_m = 2713$ kg/m$^3$, $E = 68.9$ GPa |
| Poisson's ratio | 0.33 (not given in the specification) |
| Transient field | $B_y(t) = B_0 e^{-t/\tau}$, $B_0 = 0.055$ T, $\tau = 6.6$ ms |
| Steady field | $B_x = 0.2$, 0.5, 0.7 or 0.9 T |
| Uncoupled cases | $B_x = 0$, with copper ($58.14 \times 10^{6}$ S/m), aluminium and brass ($16.39 \times 10^{6}$ S/m) |
| Measured | deflection at the free end and 167 mm from it; the circulating current |
| Simulated interval | 0 to 150 ms (0 to 40 ms for the uncoupled cases) |

With $B_x = 0$ the Lorentz force lies in the plane of the plate and the beam does not move, so these cases
isolate the eddy current solve.

## Mesh id=mesh

The domain is a hexahedral tensor-product grid generated by `team12_mesh.i`, so every feature of the problem
sits on a node plane: the edge of the clamp, mid-length, the measurement station 167 mm from the free end and
the free end itself. The beam has 10 mm elements in plan and one element through its 3.175 mm thickness, 500
in all; the surrounding free space extends 300 to 400 mm beyond it on every side and grows away from the beam,
for 15600 elements in total. There is no symmetry plane: the half-model at $z = 0$ would need a displacement
condition on one component only, which the vector Dirichlet conditions do not provide.

```bash
cd test/tests/electromagnetics/team/problem_12
<path to a MOOSE app> -i team12_mesh.i --mesh-only team12.e
```

The blocks are `Beam` and `Vacuum`. The sidesets are `left`, `right`, `bottom`, `top`, `back` and `front` on the
outer box, and `clamp`, the faces of the beam inside the clamp.

## Formulation id=formulation

Two apps solve the problem together and exchange data every time step: `team12.i` solves for the induced
magnetic field over the whole domain, and its sub-app `team12_beam.i` solves for the elastic motion of the
beam.

### Eddy Currents id=eddy-currents

The field is split as $\vec{H} = \vec{H}_\text{ext} + \vec{H}_\text{ind}$, where the applied field
$\vec{B}_\text{ext} = \mu_0 \vec{H}_\text{ext} = (B_x, B_0 e^{-t/\tau}, 0)$ is uniform, so the whole current
density is $\vec{J} = \nabla \times \vec{H}_\text{ind}$. In a moving conductor Ohm's law reads
$\vec{J} = \sigma (\vec{E} + \vec{v} \times \vec{B})$, and Faraday's law gives the weak form solved for
$\vec{H}_\text{ind}$ in $H(\text{curl})$:

!equation
\left( \mu \frac{\partial \vec{H}_\text{ind}}{\partial t}, \vec{H}' \right)
+ \left( \rho \nabla \times \vec{H}_\text{ind}, \nabla \times \vec{H}' \right)
= -\left( \frac{\partial \vec{B}_\text{ext}}{\partial t}, \vec{H}' \right)
+ \left( \vec{v} \times \vec{B}_\text{ext}, \nabla \times \vec{H}' \right)

The motional EMF $\vec{v} \times \vec{B}_\text{ext}$ is confined to the beam, so it jumps at the beam's surface.
Projecting it onto $H(\text{curl})$ would smear that jump over the surface edges; the last term instead applies it
directly in its weak form, with `MFEMVectorFEDomainLFCurlKernel`.

Free space is given a resistivity of $1\ \Omega$ m, more than seven orders of magnitude above the beam, to keep
the curl-curl operator invertible, and the tangential induced field is set to zero on the outer boundary.

The eddy currents' own field is a few millitesla at most, against a steady field of at least 0.2 T, so both the
motional EMF and the Lorentz force are evaluated with the applied field alone.

### Beam Motion id=beam-motion

The beam is linear elastic and is driven by the Lorentz force density
$\vec{f} = \vec{J} \times \vec{B}_\text{ext}$, whose $y$-component $J_z B_x$ bends it. The elastodynamic equation
is second order in time, so it is solved as a first order system in the displacement $\vec{u}$ and the velocity
$\vec{v}$, both vector $H^1$ fields of second order:

!equation
\left( \rho_m \frac{\partial \vec{v}}{\partial t}, \vec{w} \right) + \left( \sigma(\vec{u}), \nabla \vec{w} \right)
= \left( \vec{f}, \vec{w} \right),
\qquad
\left( \frac{\partial \vec{u}}{\partial t}, \vec{z} \right) - \left( \vec{v}, \vec{z} \right) = 0.

The momentum balance is tested with the displacement test functions so that the elasticity operator sits on the
diagonal block; the inertia term and the kinematic relation couple the two fields through
`MFEMTimeDerivativeVectorMassKernel` and `MFEMVectorMassKernel`. The displacement and velocity are held at zero on
`clamp`. Second order elements are used because linear hexahedra lock in bending with one element through the
thickness. The system is indefinite and small, so it is solved directly with MUMPS.

The mechanics lives on a submesh holding only the `Beam` block; the force and velocity are moved between it and
the whole mesh with `MFEMSubMeshTransfer`.

!listing canary/test/tests/electromagnetics/team/problem_12/team12_beam.i

### Coupling id=coupling

The coupling is staggered. Each step, `team12.i` evaluates the motional EMF from the beam velocity at the end
of the previous step, solves for the induced field, and sends the Lorentz force density to `team12_beam.i`,
which then advances the beam and returns its new velocity. The lag this introduces is one step of
$10^{-4}$ s, against a beam period of 65 ms and a current rise time of about 2 ms.

## Running id=running

The main app launches the sub-app itself, so only one input needs to be given. The steady field defaults to
0.5 T and is set from the command line:

```bash
mpiexec -n 4 <path to a MOOSE app built with MFEM support> -i team12.i steady_field=0.9
```

The uncoupled cases leave the sub-app out and change the conductivity:

```bash
mpiexec -n 4 <app> -i team12.i steady_field=0 beam_conductivity=58.14e6 \
  "MultiApps/active=''" "Transfers/active=''" Executioner/end_time=0.04
```

or, from the repository root, through the test harness:

```bash
./run_tests --re TEAM_Problem_12 --heavy -p 4
```

Only a 5 ms run of the coupled problem is a regular test; the seven full comparisons with experiment are
marked heavy. On four processes each coupled case takes about 45 minutes and each uncoupled case about 3.5
minutes. Almost all of the coupled cost is the direct solve of the beam's displacement-velocity system, which
is refactored every step.

Results are written to `OutputData/TEAM12CSV*.csv`, which hold the time histories of three postprocessors:

- `TipDeflection` and `StationDeflection`, the $y$-displacement on the centreline of the beam at the free end
  and 167 mm from it, sampled in the sub-app and passed up to the main app;
- `CirculatingCurrent`, the current through half of the beam's cross-section, $z > 0$, at mid-length. It is
  the volume integral of $J_x$ over the two element layers either side of mid-length divided by their 19.4 mm
  length, which with $\nabla \cdot \vec{J} = 0$ is exactly the mean of the section current over that slab.

## Results id=results

The measurements were digitised from Figs. 5, 8 and 9 of the LSTC test case document, which reproduces them
from Turner, Hua and Lee (1986); they are in `team12_experiment.csv`. The specification does not fix the sense of
$B_x$ relative to the measured deflection, and reversing $B_x$ reverses the deflection and nothing else, so the
deflections and currents below are drawn with their first swing positive, as the measurements are.

### Uncoupled id=uncoupled

With no steady field the beam stays put and the current is set by the conductivity of the plate alone.

!media media/team12_uncoupled.png
       id=uncoupled-current
       caption=The circulating current in copper, aluminium and brass beams with no steady field. A better
               conductor carries a larger current that peaks later and decays more slowly.
       style=width:80%;margin-left:auto;margin-right:auto;

| Material | Peak current, MOOSE | Peak current, measured |
| :- | -: | -: |
| Copper | 991 A at 4.4 ms | 992 A at 4.5 ms |
| Aluminium | 555 A at 2.7 ms | 595 A at 2.3 ms |
| Brass | 395 A at 2.0 ms | 391 A at 2.3 ms |

Copper and brass agree with the measured peaks to within 1%. Aluminium falls 7% short of its measured peak,
though it follows the decay closely; the converged value is about 567 A (see [Convergence](#convergence)),
against the 565 A that LS-DYNA reports for the same case, so the gap is common to both codes rather than a
property of this discretisation.

### Coupled id=coupled

!media media/team12_coupled.png
       id=coupled-results
       caption=Deflection at the free end and 167 mm from it (top) and circulating current (bottom), for each
               steady field. Lines are MOOSE, markers the FELIX measurements.
       style=width:100%;

| $B_x$ | Peak tip deflection, MOOSE | measured | Peak deflection 167 mm from the tip, MOOSE | measured |
| :- | -: | -: | -: | -: |
| 0.2 T | 5.98 mm | 6.4 mm | 2.73 mm | 2.9 mm |
| 0.5 T | 11.60 mm | 12.0 mm | 5.25 mm | 5.4 mm |
| 0.7 T | 12.80 mm | 13.5 mm | 5.80 mm | 6.1 mm |
| 0.9 T | 12.68 mm | 13.9 mm | 5.83 mm | 6.4 mm |

The coupling the benchmark exists to test is reproduced. At 0.2 T the beam rings at its first bending frequency,
about 15 Hz, and is still in phase with the measurements after two periods; at 0.5 T the second swing is
visibly damped; at 0.7 and 0.9 T the beam makes a single swing and settles. Raising $B_x$ from 0.5 to 0.9 T
raises the peak deflection by under 10%, where the uncoupled estimate would raise it by 80%. The current shows
the same interaction from the other side: the motion through $B_x$ drives a second current peak, and a dip below
zero before it, both of which grow with the steady field as measured.

The peak deflections are 3% to 9% below the measurements, the gap widening with $B_x$. Part of that follows the
aluminium current, which is about 5% low in the uncoupled case too, and part is the reading of single digitised
points at the peaks.

## Convergence id=convergence

Both checks use the uncoupled aluminium case, which carries the eddy current solve without the cost of the
mechanics, and the peak current, over the first 6 ms.

| $\Delta t$ (s) | Peak current (A) |
| :- | -: |
| $1 \times 10^{-4}$ | 555.3 |
| $5 \times 10^{-5}$ | 560.1 |
| $2.5 \times 10^{-5}$ | 562.5 |
| Richardson extrapolation | 564.9 |

Successive halvings change the peak by 4.8 and 2.4 A, so the time error is first order, as implicit Euler should
give, and the shipped $\Delta t = 10^{-4}$ s is 1.7% short of the limit. The same step damps the beam's first
mode artificially by about 7% in amplitude over 150 ms, which is small beside the electromagnetic damping in all
but the 0.2 T case.

One uniform refinement of the mesh, to eight times as many elements, moves the peak current from 555.3 to 557.2
A, 0.3%. The mechanics is converged through the thickness: with a second element through it the static
deflection of the beam under a uniform load changes by less than 0.2%.
